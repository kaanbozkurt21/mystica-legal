Terms

What you are buying, what happens if it goes wrong, and what Mystica is not. Written from the code, like the privacy policy.

Mystica is entertainment

This is the term that matters most, so it is first. Mystica writes readings from astrology, tarot, coffee grounds, palms and dreams. It is for entertainment. Every reading screen says so, and so does this page.

It is not medical, legal, financial or psychological advice, and nothing in it is a prediction of events. Do not make a decision you would not otherwise make because a reading suggested it. The app is built to refuse the worst version of itself — it will not predict death, illness or disaster, and it will not tell you to leave someone — but that is a design constraint, not a substitute for your own judgement.

It is not a crisis service. If you write something that sounds like you are in danger, Mystica stops being a fortune teller and says so plainly. That is the most it can do. If you are in trouble, talk to a person who can help.

What is free

  • Your daily horoscope, written from your own birth chart rather than your star sign.
  • The card of the day.
  • One long reading a week — a cup, a palm or a dream.

Those stay free. A subscription changes how often, not whether.

Subscription

$49.99 a year or $7.99 a week, with a 3-day free trial. It renews by itself until you cancel, and it is charged to your Apple Account. Cancel any time in Settings › your Apple Account › Subscriptions; cancelling during the trial costs nothing. Cancel at least 24 hours before a period ends, or the next one is charged.

A subscription gives you a long reading every day instead of every week, your whole birth chart, no waiting, and 10 credits a month. Those credits stack — an unused month is not lost.

Credits

Credits open the things that cost extra, like the Celtic Cross. They are sold in packs — 5 for $2.99, 12 for $5.99, 30 for $12.99 — and they are a one-off purchase: nothing renews and nothing is cancelled.

Every grant and every spend is written to a ledger that is only ever added to, so a disputed balance can be explained line by line. Credits do not expire on their own.

Deleting your account destroys unspent credits. They were bought with real money and there is no account left to restore them to — and because Mystica has no login, there is nothing for support to find either. The app says this before you tap, and it is repeated here because it is the term most likely to surprise someone.

When a reading does not arrive

If Mystica charges you a credit and then cannot produce the reading, the charge is reversed. That is written into the database rather than left to goodwill.

A cup or a palm reading is delivered after a wait, on purpose — it is being read, not generated while you watch. You can buy your way out of the wait with a credit. That buys the time, not a better reading, and the app says so on the screen where you spend it.

Refunds

Purchases go through the App Store, so refunds are Apple's to give and Apple's process to follow.

Where Mystica refunds a credit spent on a reading, the reading is voided — you do not keep the reading and the credit. That is deliberate, and it is the only shape a refund takes here.

What you write

The question before a spread and the dream in the journal are yours. Mystica stores them to write the reading from, erases them after 30 days, and keeps the reading. The privacy policy covers this in full.

Do not use those fields to send anything unlawful, and do not use Mystica to give anyone else a reading they have not asked for.

Changes

If these terms change in a way that affects what you have paid for, that change applies from the next renewal rather than retroactively. The date at the top of this page is the date it last changed.
